# When to use each formula — fit by campaign, offer, and ICP

Research-backed selection guide for P.C. / P.E.C. / P.P.C. Read this **before** picking a
formula for a campaign. Sources at the bottom; LGJ's own campaign data is cited inline.

---

## 1. The single most important finding

Benchmark data across hook types (reply rate / positive-reply rate / meeting rate):

| Hook type | Reply | Positive | Meeting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timeline** (recency, "still hasn't", "just did") | 10.01% | 65.36% | 2.34% |
| **Numbers** (a measured figure about them) | 8.57% | 61.76% | 1.86% |
| **Social proof** (case study, named client) | 6.53% | 53.44% | 1.25% |
| **Problem** (generic pain statement) | **4.39%** | 48.30% | 0.69% |

**A generic problem hook is the worst-performing hook there is — 2.3× behind timeline.**

This is the load-bearing rule for this whole skill: *pain-first only wins when the pain is a
timestamped event or a measured number.* "Are you struggling with lead flow?" is a Problem
hook and it loses. "Your team was spending on a Clutch sponsorship" is a Timeline hook and it
wins. Same formula, 2× difference.

**Therefore, on every formula:** make the pain line carry a **number**, a **recency marker**,
or both. "still hasn't found a digital sales manager" (timeline) · "worse than 87.6% of your
competitors" (numbers) · "like we just did for MailChimp" (timeline) · "3 more ideas"
(numbers). The reference examples all do this — it's not decoration.

Corollary: if you cannot attach a number or a date to the pain, **you do not have a
pain-first campaign.** Pick a different angle rather than shipping a Problem hook.

## 2. Signal availability decides the formula

| What you have per prospect | Reply rate you can expect | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| A buying signal / trigger (hiring, funding, launch, tech change, visible spend) | 15–25% for signal-triggered outreach; 5–18% typical | **P.C.** or **P.P.C.** |
| A measurable defect you can audit at scale (page speed, reviews, ad copy, directory profile) | Same tier — numbers hook, 8.57% | **P.C.** or **P.P.C.** |
| No signal, but a tight vertical + named comparable client | Social-proof hook, 6.53% | **P.E.C.** |
| No signal, no case study | Generic templates: <3% | **None of these.** Fix targeting first. |

Deep personalization without a trigger yields only modest lift over generic — signals supply
the *timing* that personalization alone cannot. Prioritize signal-scrapeable ICPs when
choosing a list.

**LGJ's own confirmation:** C1 (IT/MSP, deep `{THEIR_AUDIENCE}` / `{THEIR_SERVICE}` per-lead
variables) hit **1.44% interested**; C2 (staffing, `{COMPANY}` + `{FIRST_NAME}` only) hit
**0.23%** — a **6× gap on the same offer, same infra, same week**. Personalization depth is
the biggest single lever in this account's data.

## 3. Fit by offer type and price

| Offer | Why | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Self-serve / low-ticket (<$500), free trial, tool | Small ask, small email. Substance would be overbuilt. | **P.C.** |
| Considered purchase ($1k–$10k), category the buyer already knows | They need proof someone like them bought it | **P.E.C.** |
| Expertise-led service — agency, consulting, DFY ($5k+) | The expertise *is* the product, so demonstrate it; claims are discounted | **P.P.C.** |
| Data/list/lead products | Give a sample — the product is its own proof | **P.P.C.** |
| New category / no comparables yet | Nothing to name-drop; teach instead | **P.P.C.** |
| Regulated / enterprise procurement | Needs credibility framing before any claim | **P.E.C.**, or none of these |

**Rule of thumb:** the middle beat should scale with the size of the ask. Asking for a $97
signup with a case study is over-engineered; asking for a $10k DFY engagement with a two-line
P.C. is under-engineered.

**LGJ's own confirmation:** the DFY funnel (high-ticket) that works is value-first — *"I can
pull you a list of 500 [their ideal clients], on me"* — which is P.P.C. shaped. Consulti's
value-first openers beat cohort/feedback asks **~2.4× on identical infrastructure** (0.38% vs
0.15%). Ask-first framing has repeatedly lost in this account. Do not revive it.

## 4. Fit by ICP

| Audience | What the data says | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **CEO / Founder / Owner** | 7.63% avg reply; timeline hook 10.44% vs problem hook 4.26% | Any — but the pain line **must** be timeline or numbers |
| **C-suite generally** | 6.4% vs 5.2% non-C-suite (+23%) | Short. **P.C.** or **P.P.C.** |
| **Marketers / agencies / anyone who sends cold email** | Pattern-match templates instantly; claims discounted | **P.P.C.** — only giving cuts through |
| **Technical buyers (CTO/VP Eng, 7.68%)** | Respond to specifics and measurements | **P.C.** data/audit variant |
| **Consulting (7.88%), SaaS (7.42%), Healthcare (7.49%), FinServ (6.72%)** | Highest-replying industries | Any; lead with timeline/numbers |
| **Enterprise / competitive categories** | 79% of B2B buyers rely on social proof | **P.E.C.** |

**LGJ's converting profile** (281 genuine positives, Jan–Jul 2026): Tech/Software/IT **48%**,
Financial services/Accounting 17%, Marketing/Advertising agencies 13%. Titles: CEO/Founder/
Owner **45%**, other C-suite 21% — **66% top decision-makers**, skewing SMB/solopreneur.
That audience is (a) small enough to have no procurement, (b) sends or receives outbound
daily, (c) buys on demonstrated competence. **P.P.C. is the native fit for this account's
core ICP.**

## 5. Length — where each formula sits

Benchmark: **50–125 words** is the optimal band, **under 80 words** best for a first touch;
50–125-word emails reply ~50% better than longer formats.

| Formula | Words | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **P.C.** | 35–60 | **Below the researched band.** Fine when the signal is self-evidently strong (a hard number, an obvious waste). If the pain needs any setup, it will read thin — use P.P.C. instead. |
| **P.P.C.** | 70–95 | **Dead center of the optimum.** Best structural fit of the three. |
| **P.E.C.** | 80–120 | Inside the band, at the long end. Watch the 45-word opening sentence — trim it before trimming anything else. |

## 6. CTA — why each formula asks differently

| CTA type | Total reply | Positive reply | Meeting conversion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interest-based ("Want more info?") | **12%** | **68%** | 15% (cold stage) |
| Time request ("Let's do a call") | 7% | 41% | **37% — but only once the prospect is in active evaluation** |

Interest-based CTAs roughly **2×** time-requests on cold first touches. Direct calendar asks
outperform by 2.5× **later**, after engagement. Best practice from the same research:
question-based CTA on day 1, value-first on day 3–5, direct meeting ask on day 8+.

That maps exactly onto the ladder:

- **P.C.** → interest ask. Correct for a cold first touch. Safest CTA in the family.
- **P.E.C.** → permission ask ("Mind if I send times?"). Middle ground; the case study buys it.
- **P.P.C.** → direct call ask. **This is the one element of these formulas that runs against
  the benchmark.** The free tip is what's supposed to move the prospect out of "cold" and into
  "engaged" inside a single email. It's a real bet, not a settled fact.
  - **Always A/B the P.P.C. CTA.** Test "Let's do a call this week?" against the softer
    "Want the other 3?" — the soft version keeps the interest-CTA advantage and still opens
    the loop. In this account, where value-first has beaten ask-first 2.4×, the soft variant
    is the one to bet on first.

## 7. Upgrade modifiers — apply to whichever formula you pick

Independent of formula, these move the number:

- **≤50-lead cohorts → 2.76× reply lift.** One tight segment per campaign, one tailored pain
  line, one tailored tip. Never one email for a 10k list.
- **Deep per-lead research variables → 52%+ reply lift** (LGJ's own C1 vs C2: 6×). Give every
  formula at least one variable that required actually looking at the company.
- **Add a number and a recency word** to the pain line — see §1.
- **3-7-7 cadence captures 93% of replies by day 10.** Diminishing returns after the second
  follow-up.
- **Name the specific pain rather than a benefit** — pain-led B2B emails convert ~22% better.

---

## 8. Sequence placement

These are all **email-1 shapes**. In a 3-step sequence:

| Step | Shape | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | **P.P.C.** (if you have a tip) or **P.C.** | Interest / soft |
| E2 (day 3–5) | The remaining value — deliver idea #2 unprompted, or **P.E.C.**'s case study | Permission |
| E3 (day 8+) | Direct ask + hard close ("last one, reply and it's yours") | Meeting |

LGJ's C1/C2 winner uses exactly this: value-first give at E1, escalating to a hard
reply-and-it's-yours close at E3.

**Do not repeat the same formula three times.** The CTA research is explicit that a day-1 CTA
fails at touch three.

---

## 9. When NOT to use any of them

- **No signal and no case study and no give-able tip** → the opener goes generic, you ship a
  Problem hook, and you land in the 4.39% / <3% tier. Fix the list before the copy.
- **The "pain" is a guess.** A wrong mind-read (P.E.C.) or a fabricated audit (P.C.) is worse
  than no email — one caught fabrication ends the domain's reputation with that buyer.
- **You can't fulfill the give.** P.P.C. promises N more ideas and P.P.C.-style "free list"
  offers promise real work. LGJ's C1 replies asked for **ICP filters before delivery**
  ("revenue 20M–250M, manufacturing") — if fulfillment can't handle that, the give backfires.
- **The free framing reads as rev-share.** A C1 lead replied *"What's the payment/share
  model?"* to an "on me" offer. State plainly that it's a no-cost sample with no strings.
- **Follow-ups** — see §8.
